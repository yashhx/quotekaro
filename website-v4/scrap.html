01 · Stock

A yard loses material slowly, and quietly.

Not in one dramatic night — a little on each loading, over months. Purchases are recorded, sales are recorded, and the difference between the two is assumed to be lying outside. Nobody weighs the assumption. By the time somebody does, the gap is a number no one can explain.

TrackRakho keeps a running book figure per material — opening stock, plus purchases, minus everything dispatched, including every truck. Then it asks you to weigh.

A kanta check takes ten minutes. Enter what the weighbridge actually says; the app compares it with what the books expect and shows the variance. If it is not zero, you find out this week — not next audit.

  • Material-wise book stock, kept current by the day's dispatches
  • Physical count resets the baseline, so errors do not compound forever
  • Manual "gone without a truck" entries, with a warning against double counting

02 · Fleet

Which truck went where, with how much, and is it back?

Loadings are agreed on the phone and remembered by whoever was standing at the gate. Two days later, reconstructing which vehicle carried which material to which dealer is guesswork — and the weighbridge slip is in someone's pocket.

Log a loading in seconds: truck, dealer, material, weighbridge tonnage, reference. The truck shows as OUT with the hours it has been gone, until you mark it delivered.

Every dispatch also moves the yard stock, so the fleet record and the stock record can never quietly disagree.

Three answers, one screen

Who is oldest?

Not who owes the most — who has been sitting on it the longest. Those are rarely the same party, and only one of them needs a call today.

Which bill exactly?

Asking for “ten lakh” gets a vague reply. Asking for bill #APX/PO-112 of ₹3,25,600, forty-three days old, gets a date.

Who should get the next load?

The planner ranks open orders by deadline, how long a dealer has waited, and how much is left — and slides a party down when too much of your money is already parked with them. Every reason is shown on the card; nothing is hidden in a score.
