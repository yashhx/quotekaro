The problem, as owners describe it

From visits to real shops and yards:

  • Quotations get forgotten. A quote goes out on WhatsApp, nobody follows up, and the order goes to someone else.
  • Money is stuck and nobody knows exactly where. Customers pay 60–90+ days late, and the owner cannot say which bill, how old, or how much.
  • Tally has the numbers, but Tally lives on one computer in the office. The owner is on the shop floor or at the yard.
  • Machine shops cannot tell a customer when a job will finish, or what a machine hour really costs them.
  • Scrap yards lose stock quietly. One trader we spoke to lost around 17 tonnes over six months before anyone noticed.
  • Everything important is buried in WhatsApp. Enquiries, photos of drawings, purchase orders.

The promise in one line

Your whole business on one screen — what is pending, what you have won, what money is stuck, and who to chase today.

The selling idea that works best

Owners do not buy "a nice app". They respond to money they are losing right now: the forgotten quote, the payment stuck for 72 days, the tonnes missing from the yard. Lead with the loss, then show the screen that fixes it. In live demos, the single most convincing moment is showing an owner his own overdue bills from his own Tally.

5.1 · Login & accounts

Live
BOTH TRADES · LIVE IN PRODUCTION SINCE 17 SEP 2026
What it is

Owners log in with their mobile number. A 6-digit code arrives on WhatsApp from TrackRakho's verified business number. Google sign-in is available as a second option.

How it works
  • Type your 10-digit mobile number → tap Send code → a WhatsApp message with a Copy code button arrives → paste into the six boxes → you are in.
  • You stay logged in. No code every morning — only after deliberately logging out.
  • One account, two doors: in Setup → "Ways to log in", an owner can link both his phone and his Google account. Either one opens the same shop with the same data.
  • Protection against losing data by accident: if someone who normally uses Google logs in with his phone for the first time, the app asks "Is this a new shop account?" and sends him back to his real data instead of showing an empty app.
  • Each business's data lives in its own private account. The app keeps a copy on the phone, works without internet, and syncs when the connection returns. Setup shows Synced / Saving / Offline.
Why owners care

Many owners do not have — or do not remember — a Gmail password. A mobile number and a WhatsApp code is something every one of them can do in ten seconds.

On the website: "Log in with your mobile number" is a genuine advantage for this audience — worth a line near sign-up. Say the code comes on WhatsApp, not by SMS.

5.7 · Quotation maker & true costing

Live
MACHINE SHOPS ONLY
How it works
  • A step-by-step flow: part & customer → material → machining → price. The running price is visible the whole time.
  • Price per piece = material (kg × ₹/kg) + machine time (cycle minutes × machine hourly rate, plus one-time setup spread across the quantity) + hands-on labour + tooling → plus overhead % → plus margin % = price per piece. Total = price × quantity, with GST shown.
  • True machine-hour rate calculator: depreciation + power (kW × ₹ per unit) + operator + maintenance, divided by monthly working hours. Example: a VMC 850 works out to about ₹366 an hour.
  • Built-in lists of around 19 common NCR machines and common materials (EN8, EN19, SS 304, aluminium grades…), all with editable rates.
  • Produces a clean PDF quotation to send on WhatsApp.

Important: the owner enters the cycle time — the app does the money maths around it. The app does not calculate or predict machining time. Never write copy that implies it does.

On the website: "Know what a machine hour really costs you." Show the worked example from Section 8 — it adds up exactly.

5.8 · Machine floor

Live
MACHINE SHOPS ONLY
How it works
  • Machines are set up with a count, so "VMC 850 × 2" becomes VMC 850 #1 and #2.
  • Start a job: part, customer, quantity, cycle minutes per piece, handling minutes per piece (loading, deburring). Pick the free machines; the quantity is split between them.
  • A live board shows each machine's % done, pieces done, time left and finish time ("today 6:40 pm", "tomorrow 11:00 am").
  • Every estimate includes an 8% allowance for tool changes and small breakdowns — and says so on screen.
  • Pause a machine; move the remaining pieces to another machine; all estimates recalculate.
  • Mark done to free the machine; completed jobs go to history. Won orders arrive here from the pipeline in one tap.
Why owners care

Customers ring asking "kab tak milega?" The owner can answer from his phone, from anywhere.

Estimates come from the times the owner enters. There are no sensors, no machine connections, no real-time monitoring hardware.

5.9 · Tally connector

Beta
BOTH TRADES · VALIDATED ON REAL TALLYPRIME
What it is

A small program that runs on the computer where TallyPrime is installed and sends the numbers to the owner's TrackRakho account.

Setup — about 5 minutes, 3 steps
  1. Copy a folder onto the Tally computer and double-click it. It installs what it needs by itself.
  2. Paste the key shown in the app. It checks the key and prints the shop's name back, so the owner knows it is the right account.
  3. Pick the sales ledger from a numbered list of what is actually in the books. No typing, no configuration files.

After that it starts by itself whenever the computer is switched on, with no window on screen, and keeps a readable log.

What it reads from Tally
  • Customer and supplier balances
  • Bill-by-bill outstanding with bill dates and due dates
  • The last 45 days of sales and purchase vouchers, with quantities and voucher / reference numbers
Read-only by default

It writes nothing into Tally. An optional feature can push won orders into Tally as Sales Orders — it ships switched off and stays off until the owner turns it on himself.

Proof

Validated against real TallyPrime Release 7.x, on both GST and non-GST books, matched to the rupee against Tally's own Bills Receivable report — in both directions.

On the website: "It reads your Tally. It does not touch it." This line answers the accountant's objection before he raises it. Label the connector Beta.

5.10 · Tally insights

Beta
BOTH TRADES · NEEDS THE CONNECTOR · A LABELLED SAMPLE IS SHOWN WITHOUT IT
  • "Aane wale paise" — money customers owe you, and "Dene wale paise" — money you owe suppliers. Tap either for a party-by-party breakdown with share bars and each party's last transaction.

5.11 · MSMED interest & polite reminder

Live
MACHINE SHOPS THAT ARE UDYAM-REGISTERED · SWITCHED ON IN SETUP
  • Indian law (the MSMED Act) says a registered micro or small manufacturer's buyer must pay within 45 days, or owe compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate.
  • For each overdue bill from Tally, the app shows the estimated interest the customer legally owes. The calculation is deliberately conservative — every rounding goes downward — and is always labelled an estimate.
  • "WhatsApp par narmi se yaad dilao" (remind gently on WhatsApp): a ready message that mentions the interest for information, explicitly says the business does not want to claim it, and politely asks for payment.
  • Never shown to scrap traders — traders are excluded from this law.

Tone rule: present this as knowing your rights and asking respectfully. Never "legal notice", "recover dues", "take action", or anything that reads as a threat. In Indian trade that damages the relationship.

5.13 · Yard stock & weighbridge check

Live
SCRAP TRADERS ONLY
  • Book stock for each material = opening stock (or last physical count) + purchases − sales − truck dispatches.
  • "Kanta check": weigh what is really in the yard and enter it. The app shows the shortfall (ghata) in tonnes, records it, and resets the baseline.
  • Home shows a red GHATA badge; tiles show total yard tonnage, shortfall, dispatched today and a 7-day bar chart.
  • Example: book stock 43.8 MT, weighed 42.0 MT → −1.8 MT, found this week rather than in six months.
  • The app does not convert the shortfall into rupees. If the website does, state the rate used (e.g. "about ₹59,400 at ₹33/kg").

A discipline tool based on regular weighing. No RFID, no sensors, no automatic weighbridge integration.

App look

White base, forest green, rounded cards (~22 px radius), pill-shaped buttons, a frosted-glass bottom navigation bar. Readability first: body text 15–17 px minimum, large numbers, big tap targets — the users are older owners.

Illustration rules — learned from rejected rounds

  • Draw real-world, recognisable scenes with craft: a loaded tipper truck standing on a weighbridge with its digital display, a VMC machine, a Tally screen on a monitor, a WhatsApp chat bubble.
  • No people at all — no full figures, no hands holding phones. (Tried twice; both rejected.)
  • No metaphor objects — no clouds, pockets, rockets, lightbulbs.
  • One unified scene per illustration, not layered separate elements that drift apart at different screen sizes.
  • Text never overlaps artwork — reserve a clear text area.
  • Show the real product UI in its true white and green, not a stylised version.

What the founder liked

  • Scroll-driven storytelling where scrolling reveals the story step by step (Pear-style) — without hijacking the scroll.
  • An interactive walkthrough where the visitor performs the steps (paste the enquiry, save the quote, mark it won) and each step ticks green.
  • A "everything connects to your phone" finale: four vignettes (weighbridge / truck / Tally / stock — or VMC / quotation / Tally / WhatsApp) with lines drawing into a central phone.
  • Calm, confident, enterprise-professional tone.

What was tried and rejected

  • A dark, heavy-Hinglish scrap site opening with a theft story — felt amateur.
  • A generic multi-page editorial redesign — felt ordinary.
  • Drawn people, hands, a phone going into a trouser pocket.
  • Tilted 3D phone mockups on the trade pages.
  • Stat panels full of numbers without context in the hero.

Research that should shape the new site

  • An interactive product demo is now the single most useful thing on a B2B software website — rated above pricing pages, case studies and free trials.
  • Best demos run 8–12 steps split into chapters, with outcome-led titles ("A customer messages you at 11 pm") rather than button names ("Click Save").

  • Owners will mostly visit on a phone; partners and accountants often on a laptop. Both need to work well.
